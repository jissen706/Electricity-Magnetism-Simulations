Magnetic Flux & Orientation

How the angle between B and the loop's area vector A changes the flux Φ = B·A = BA cos θ

Flux through a tilted loop

Magnetic flux Φ depends on three things: the field strength B, the loop area A, and the angle θ between B and the loop's area vector A (the unit normal to the loop face).

Φ = ∫ B · dA   →   (uniform B)   Φ = B A cos θ

Tilt the loop with the θ slider. When θ = 0° the loop face is perpendicular to B (max flux). When θ = 90° the loop is edge-on to B (zero flux). The orange highlighted field lines are the ones that actually thread the loop.

Rotating loop: Φ traces a cosine

Now spin the loop at a steady angular speed ω. Because Φ = BA cos θ and θ = ωt, the flux oscillates as a cosine. This is the setup behind every AC generator — and the time-derivative is the EMF that Faraday's law predicts.

θ(t) = ω t   ⟹   Φ(t) = B A cos(ω t)
