Free vs. Bound Charges

Conductors: In a metal, outer electrons are detached and are "free" to move through the material.

Dielectrics: In an ideal dielectric, no charges are free to move indefinitely; every electron is bound to its parent molecule.

Nucleus / positive Electron Applied E field

Polar vs. Nonpolar Molecules

Polar molecules (e.g. H₂O) have a natural dipole; an applied field applies a torque and they align with the field.

Nonpolar molecules have no permanent dipole; the field induces one by stretching the electron cloud relative to the nucleus.

Polarization and Bound Surface Charges

When the material is polarized, positive and negative charges shift in opposite directions. A thin layer of bound charge (σᵢ) builds up on each surface; the interior stays neutral.

The Opposing Electric Field

The bound surface charges create an induced field Eᵢ that points opposite to the original field E₀. The net field inside the dielectric is weaker:

E_net = E₀ − Eᵢ
