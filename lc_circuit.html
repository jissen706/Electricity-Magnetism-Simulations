LC Circuit Oscillations

An ideal capacitor and inductor trade energy back and forth — charge, current, voltage, and stored energy each oscillate with their own personality.

Charge on C and current through L are 90° out of phase

Start with charge Q₀ on the capacitor and zero current. The capacitor pushes current into the inductor; the inductor's growing current keeps flowing even after the capacitor empties, recharging it the other way. The cycle repeats forever (no resistance).

q(t) = Q₀ cos(ω t)     i(t) = dq/dt = −Q₀ ω sin(ω t)     ω = 1/√(LC)

When q is at its peak, i = 0 (charge sits still on the plates). When q = 0, i is at its peak (charge is mid-flight through the inductor). They are exactly a quarter period apart.
ω =— rad/s f =— Hz T =— ms i_max =— mA q(t) =— μC i(t) =— mA
q(t) — charge on capacitor i(t) — current through inductor

Kirchhoff: at every instant V_C = V_L

Going around the loop, the voltage drop across the capacitor equals the back-EMF of the inductor. So VC(t) and VL(t) trace the same curve — they oscillate in phase with q(t), not with i(t). Their peak values both equal Q₀/C.

V_C(t) = q(t)/C     V_L(t) = L·di/dt = q(t)/C     V_max = Q₀/C

The two curves overlap exactly. That isn't a coincidence — it's the loop rule applied to a circuit with no resistor, no battery, and no other element. If you change L, V_max doesn't change; if you change C, both curves rescale together.
V_max = Q₀/C =— V T =— ms V_C =— V V_L =— V
V_C(t) = q/C V_L(t) = L·di/dt

Energy sloshes between C and L at twice the frequency

Capacitor energy UC = q²/(2C) and inductor energy UL = ½Li² are both squared quantities, so each oscillates at 2ω. They are perfectly complementary: when one is full, the other is empty. Their sum is constant.

U_C(t) = q²/(2C) = U₀ cos²(ωt)     U_L(t) = ½Li² = U₀ sin²(ωt)     U₀ = Q₀²/(2C)

Energy oscillates twice as fast as charge. cos²(ωt) + sin²(ωt) = 1 isn't just trig — it's energy conservation. The ideal LC tank loses nothing; the same U₀ keeps moving back and forth.
U₀ = Q₀²/(2C) =— μJ T_energy = T/2 =— ms U_C =— μJ U_L =— μJ U_total =— μJ
U_C — capacitor (electric) U_L — inductor (magnetic) U_total — conserved
