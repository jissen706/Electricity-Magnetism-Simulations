Lenz's Law

The induced current flows in whatever direction creates a magnetic field that opposes the change in flux.

Move a magnet toward a loop

Push the bar magnet toward the loop and watch what the loop does. Flux through the loop changes, so a current is induced — and its direction is whatever makes the loop's own magnetic field push back against that change.

ε = −dΦ/dt   ⟹   I flows in the direction whose B-field opposes ΔΦ

As the N pole approaches: the loop's induced current makes the near face act like an N pole — pushing the magnet away. Pull it back out and the loop becomes an S pole — pulling the magnet back. Either way, the loop "fights" the change.

Slide a loop through a B-field region

The blue rectangle is a region of uniform B pointing into the page (the × marks). Slide the loop across it. Current only flows while the flux is changing — so only at the entry and exit edges. Inside the region (and fully outside) the flux is constant: no EMF, no current.

Φ = B · A_inside(x)   ⟹   I ∝ −dΦ/dt

Entering: flux into the page is growing. The loop fights it by driving a CCW current — that current's B (right-hand rule) points out of the page. Exiting: flux is shrinking. The loop tries to keep it, so current reverses to CW — its B points into the page.
